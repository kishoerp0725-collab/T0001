AI employees vs AI agents:
which does your team need?

Most teams mix them up. The difference decides whether your automation runs a task once — or does a job over time.

AI agentAI employee
ScopeOne task, one runA role with ongoing responsibilities
LifecycleStarts and stops per promptWatches, follows up, acts over time
KnowledgeWhatever you paste inRemembers your accounts, clients & thresholds
AccountabilityYou re-trigger itOwns its queue, escalates when stuck
Typical example"Summarise this invoice""Chase every overdue invoice over LKR 500k"

What is an AI employee?

An AI employee is an agent with a job description. It has a name, a scope, a set of connected apps (your ERPNext instance, Gmail, Slack), and approval thresholds. It doesn't wait for a prompt — it watches its queue and acts. When something crosses your threshold, it pauses and asks you.

Think of Arthur, the AR collector: he scans open invoices every morning, sends personalised reminders, and holds anything over LKR 500,000 for your sign-off. You don't tell him to run — he just does the job.

What is an AI agent?

An AI agent executes a single instruction well and stops. "Reconcile this bank statement" — done, then idle. Agents are perfect for one-off jobs and are actually the building blocks inside every AI employee.

Where the "employee" idea helps — and where it breaks

It helps when the work is continuous: collections, support triage, lead follow-up, content calendars. The naming matters because it sets expectations — you hire a role, not a script.

It breaks when the work is truly one-off. For that, use a plain agent task. We don't force the employee metaphor where it doesn't fit.
